# Evidence boundaries for Work & career and Everyday life reports

Reviewed: 2026-10-11. Product language: English. All proposed report prose and exercises are original editorial content. No competitor reports were copied.

## What the report can reasonably provide

The assessment measures self-described personality tendencies. A useful report connects those answers to situations the reader can recognize, then proposes a small experiment. The report must keep three layers visible: scored observation, tentative interpretation, and optional action. Published associations support topics worth discussing; they do not prove that a specific exercise will work for this person.

Example structure: “Your answers show stronger endorsement of planning and follow-through statements. You may find a visible plan useful when several tasks compete. For one week, choose one priority before opening your inbox and note whether starting becomes easier.” The last sentence is a suggested experiment, not a measured outcome or a validated treatment.

## Content map and limits

The following is an editorial mapping from item content to reflective topics. It is not a new validation study and does not imply that the instrument directly measures all outcomes mentioned.

| Measured domain | Work & career discussion | Everyday life discussion | Inference to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Openness | Trying alternative approaches, comfort with unfamiliar ideas, preference for variety versus familiar procedures | Learning interests, creative exploration and adjusting routines | Intelligence score, political affiliation prediction, creativity guarantee or a particular profession |
| Conscientiousness | Planning, starting tasks, keeping commitments, checking decisions and pacing effort | Household systems, small routines and realistic commitments | Competence, moral worth, ADHD diagnosis, future earnings or productivity guarantee |
| Extraversion | Preferred interaction frequency, speaking up, meeting format, stimulation and pace | Social contact, activity choices and room for quiet time | Leadership ability, social skill, popularity, loneliness or an introvert/extrovert binary |
| Agreeableness | Giving feedback, handling disagreements, balancing cooperation with boundaries | Requests, saying no, trust and everyday disagreements | Being a good/bad person, relationship compatibility, honesty certification or abuse screening |
| Neuroticism | Noticing stress, reacting to setbacks, preparation and recovery between demands | Worry, emotional reactions and noticing when support would help | Anxiety/depression diagnosis, emotional weakness, trauma history or a mental-health risk score |

Neuroticism is the research term; a plain-language label such as “Sensitivity to stress” can help if the mapping is disclosed. Facet names such as Depression, Morality, Intellect and Liberalism need short explanations so users do not mistake them for diagnoses, moral judgments, IQ, or a complete political profile. Keep the original source name available.

## Interpretation rules

- Describe “your answers” rather than asserting access to a hidden true self. Preserve variation across time, roles and situations.
- Give balanced consequences for either end of a scale, without flattering every score or presenting high as universally better.
- A middle score can reflect moderate endorsement or a mix of different facet scores. Show that mix where it materially changes interpretation.
- Do not invent interactions: two elevated scores support two observations, not a scientifically established personality type. Name each score behind any combined paragraph.
- Do not infer values, motives, abilities, interests in specific careers, attachment style or a partner's personality from Big Five answers alone.
- Do not make claims that percentile ranks exist when only raw scale positions are available. “Near the middle of this answer scale” is different from “average for people.”
- Avoid treating a 1-point display difference as meaningful. Four-item facets deserve especially tentative wording.
- The user must be able to revise an answer and receive a consistently updated report, including exports.

## Suggested action library

These are low-stakes editorial experiments, not empirically validated personalized interventions. Each should state the score/facet prompting it and invite the reader to keep, adjust or discard it.

| Topic | Example experiment | Reflection question |
|---|---|---|
| Structure | Select one visible priority and a ten-minute starting step for three workdays. | Did the smaller start help, or was a different obstacle more important? |
| Flexibility | Try one alternative approach to a low-risk recurring task. | Was the novelty useful enough to keep? |
| Social pace | Compare a short conversation with a written update for one routine collaboration. | Which format helped you contribute with less friction? |
| Boundaries | For one new request, state your available time before accepting. | Did this make expectations clearer? |
| Disagreement | Summarize the other person's point before giving one concrete concern. | Did both sides understand the disagreement better? |
| Pressure | After a demanding event, note the trigger, your response and one controllable next step. | Which part of the situation could you prepare for next time? |

Choose three actions from the user's pattern, avoid duplication, and allow choice. An action should never require disclosing scores to an employer or partner. Work and everyday chapters can share a measured tendency while using distinct examples.

## Review and release criteria

Every personalized paragraph needs an explicit domain/facet dependency and a defined interpretation band. Test profiles at both ends, midpoint, boundary values and conflicting facets. Verify that the same inputs produce the same report and that copied/downloaded reports preserve score explanations and limitations. Do not call the report scientifically validated just because the underlying inventory has published evidence.

The local product can be complete as a self-reflection implementation while commercial launch still requires decisions about audience, market, privacy, price and payment fulfillment. Population norms, clinical use, recruitment use, country-specific interpretation and efficacy of the action library remain outside what this research establishes.
